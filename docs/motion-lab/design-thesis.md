# Processing Motion Lab — design thesis

## Structural concepts

### A. Observatory grid

1. **Foreground:** a large status clock and a field of ten simultaneous instruments.
2. **Sequence:** lab context → global controls → ten equal experiments → evaluation note.
3. **Action:** replay and pause sit with the status clock because they manipulate the whole field.
4. **Responsive transformation:** the field moves from two columns to one; controls wrap without reordering the experiments.
5. **Risk:** simultaneous movement becomes visual noise and prevents close evaluation.

### B. Screening room

1. **Foreground:** one selected animation fills the viewport.
2. **Sequence:** selected experiment → stage explanation → thumbnail strip → controls.
3. **Action:** next/previous selection dominates because comparison is serial.
4. **Responsive transformation:** thumbnails become a horizontal rail on narrow screens.
5. **Risk:** serial inspection hides comparative differences in rhythm and silhouette.

### C. Contact sheet with focus mode

1. **Foreground:** a calm two-column contact sheet; a concept can expand in place later.
2. **Sequence:** lab context → controls → comparative cards → optional focused inspection.
3. **Action:** global controls stay primary; cards earn attention through their animation, not buttons.
4. **Responsive transformation:** cards stack and internal geometry compresses; no carousel or hidden content.
5. **Risk:** glass treatment could make ten concepts look more similar than they are.

## Selected thesis

Use concept C without expansion in the first prototype. The governing idea is: **the same invisible processing job should become ten different, legible physical transformations**. A familiar dashboard/contact-sheet backbone makes comparison instant. The signature departures are the oversized laboratory status display and ten structurally different animation stages rendered as translucent instruments.

Abandon the direction if the first five seconds read as “ten fancy spinners,” if global motion competes with labels, or if a 700 px-tall window cannot reach and operate the controls normally.

## Attention path

1. “PROCESSING / MOTION LAB” establishes experimental context.
2. The live stage clock explains that the field is synchronized.
3. The eye compares the first row's transformation silhouettes.
4. Repeated numbered labels make scanning the remaining concepts predictable.
5. Global replay, pause, speed, and reduced-motion controls invite evaluation.

## Responsive transformation

- Desktop: two-column contact sheet with generous internal canvases.
- Short/narrow: one column, tighter card chrome, animation geometry remains at full conceptual fidelity.
- Mobile-width evidence is for lab robustness only; the clinical app remains desktop-only.

## References

No external visual reference was selected. Structure, typography, surface, and motion derive from the supplied product and lab constraints.
